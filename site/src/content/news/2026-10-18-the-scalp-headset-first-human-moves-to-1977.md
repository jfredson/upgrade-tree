## What happened

In May 1977 [Jacques Vidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain%E2%80%93computer_interface#History), at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), published [a paper in the Proceedings of the IEEE](https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1977.10542) in which volunteers with electrodes on the scalp, and no surgery, moved a symbol through a simple maze on a screen in real time. Each chose a direction by looking at one of four dots around a flashing checkerboard, and the computer sorted the brain's response to each flash into one of the four directions.

The site had known of the paper since its [checks of 17 October 2026](/story/#2026-10-17), but only its abstract could be read, so the question of whether it met the test was put to John and left open. On 18 October 2026 John bought the paper, it was read page by page, and he ruled the same day that it counts ([the upgrade's page](/branches/link/link-scalp-headset-controls-a-device-without-surgery/)).

## Why

The upgrade's test asks for a person, with no surgery, using signals read from the scalp to control a device. Vidal's volunteers did that: the paper reports the computer picking the right one of four directions more than 90 per cent of the time with average subjects ([the paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1977.10542), page 638). A study on this site is dated by its publication, and the paper dates none of its sessions, so the rung is dated to the issue it appeared in, May 1977. The [1988 Illinois report](https://doi.org/10.1016/0013-4694(88)90149-6), in which volunteers spelled words by attending to flashing letters, was the first-human record until now and stays in the upgrade's notes.

## What it does not mean

It does not mean anyone wore a headset to control a device outside a laboratory in 1977. The paper gives no account of a volunteer getting out of the maze, and it does not test whether the choice depended on the eyes moving toward the dot rather than on the brain's response alone ([the upgrade's page](/branches/link/link-scalp-headset-controls-a-device-without-surgery/) carries both cautions). And the count of upgrades past first human does not move, because the headset was already past that rung ([the branches](/branches/)).

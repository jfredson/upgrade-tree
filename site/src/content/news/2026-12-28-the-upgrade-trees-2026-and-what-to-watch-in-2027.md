A child born without a working copy of one gene, called OTOF, cannot hear. The ear is built, but the cells that catch sound cannot pass it on to the nerve. On April 23rd, 2026, the United States [Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/) approved Otarmeni, made by [Regeneron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneron_Pharmaceuticals), which puts a working copy of that gene into the inner ear in a treatment given once. Otarmeni is the first gene therapy approved for a hearing loss passed down in families, and in the trial behind the approval, 80 per cent of the 20 patients whose results could be judged heard better ([the approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-ever-gene-therapy-treatment-genetic-hearing-loss-under-national-priority-voucher)).

[The Upgrade Tree](/) is a site that follows 35 ways of changing a human being with technology, and places each one on a ladder of five stages: idea only, shown in animals, first human (tried in at least one living person), in clinical use, and in wide use. When the site opened on October 16th, 2026, its first check of the news found the approval, and that day the hearing gene therapy moved up from first human to in clinical use ([the upgrade's page](/branches/senses/senses-gene-therapy-restores-hearing-in-a-child-born-deaf/)). No other change on the site has moved up a stage since it opened.

## The rest of the year

**A speech implant went home.** In June 2026 a team at the [University of California, Davis](https://health.ucdavis.edu/) reported a man with [motor neurone disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amyotrophic_lateral_sclerosis) who used an implant in the speech area of his brain at home, with no researcher in the room, for nearly two years. He tries to speak, and words appear on a screen. Over that time he produced more than 183,000 sentences at an average of 56 words a minute ([UC Davis Health](https://health.ucdavis.edu/medical-school-research/news/headlines/brain-computer-interface-enables-independent-accurate-communication-for-man-living-with-als/2026/06); [the paper in Nature Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04414-6)). Fifty-six words a minute is slower than the fastest checked figure for such an implant, 78 words a minute in a laboratory in 2023 ([the chart](/branches/link/#bandwidth)), but the study is the first long record of a speech implant as part of a life rather than an experiment. On the site, speech implants stay at first human, because the next stage, in clinical use, needs a regulator's approval ([the upgrade's page](/branches/link/link-brain-implant-turns-intended-speech-into-text/)).

**A pig kidney kept a man off dialysis for 271 days.** A man in Boston who received a kidney from a gene-edited pig in January 2025 lived on it for 271 days before it was replaced with a human kidney, reported in the medical journal [The Lancet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lancet) in September 2026 ([NBC News](https://www.nbcboston.com/news/national-international/pig-kidney-record-271-days-human-body/4009901/)). That is a record for a pig kidney in a living person. On the site, organs from gene-edited pigs stay at first human, because the next stage needs a regulator's approval for use outside a trial ([the upgrade's page](/branches/lifespan/lifespan-organ-from-a-gene-edited-pig-keeps-a-person-alive/)).

**The first person was given a treatment that partly resets cells.** Four genes switched on briefly can turn back some of the marks of age in mice. On June 9th, 2026, [Life Biosciences](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/) said it had treated the first person with ER-100, a gene therapy that does this in the eye, for people losing sight to [glaucoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaucoma) ([the company](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/life-biosciences-announces-first-patient-dosed-in-phase-1-trial-of-er-100-for-optic-neuropathies/)). On October 8th it reported that two of the first three people did better on a test of the field of vision after 56 days ([the company's results](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/life-biosciences-announces-first-in-human-data-from-ongoing-phase-1-trial-evaluating-er-100-in-optic-neuropathies/)). Those are the company's own figures, with no untreated group to compare. For this change to count as tried in a first human, the site asks for a sign of age measurably turned back in a person, in a report checked by other scientists, because treating someone does not show that ageing was reversed. So the site keeps this change at shown in animals ([the upgrade's page](/branches/lifespan/lifespan-partially-resetting-cells-reverses-ageing-in-an-animal/)).

**Chatbots became a daily habit for one American in four.** A survey by the [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/), taken in February 2026 and published on June 17th, found that about half of American adults use an AI chatbot, and roughly one in four use one every day ([the survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026/)). The site counts a mind that routinely thinks with a machine as a changed mind, and what it asks for is a survey showing that most adults in one country use such an assistant every day. No survey has shown that yet ([the upgrade's page](/branches/mind/mind-ai-assistant-used-daily-by-most-adults-in-a-country/)).

**A brain-implant company's owner made claims that its published figures do not show.** [Neuralink](https://neuralink.com/) said in January 2026 that 21 people were enrolled in its trials worldwide ([the company](https://neuralink.com/updates/two-years-of-telepathy/)), and in October that one of them had moved a cursor at 11.32 bits a second, a measure of how much information gets through ([the company](https://neuralink.com/updates/pretraining-on-50000-hours/)). Neither figure has been checked by other scientists. On October 30th its owner, [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk), wrote that Neuralink's implant would soon carry information faster than speech, and that the company's implant for the blind was ready. Both posts went into the site's log of claims, where each is set beside the published evidence, which so far shows neither ([the post of October 31st](/news/2026-10-31-musks-claims-for-telepathy-and-blindsight-beside-the-record/)).

## What the site found since it opened

The site's main number counts how many of the 35 changes it follows have been tried in at least one living person. It stood at 28 of 35 when the site opened in October, and it stands at 28 of 35 now ([the count](/data/headline.json)). Every week the site checks the news for each change, and in the first five checks only the hearing therapy moved up a stage ([this week's check](/reading/)).

One of the site's finds was about the past. The earliest record it had of a person working a computer through electrodes on the scalp, with no surgery, was a speller from 1988. A paper by [Jacques Vidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain%E2%80%93computer_interface#History) published in May 1977 shows volunteers at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://www.ucla.edu/) steering a symbol through a maze on a screen with their brain signals, and on October 18th the site moved its date for the first person to work a device through the scalp from 1988 back to 1977, eleven years earlier ([the paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1977.10542); [the post](/news/2026-10-18-the-scalp-headset-first-human-moves-to-1977/)).

## Coming up in 2027

Most of these dates are companies' hopes or a trial registry's estimates, and such dates slip often.

**A decision on psilocybin therapy in the United States.** [Compass Pathways](https://compasspathways.com/) finished its application to the Food and Drug Administration for COMP360, a treatment using [psilocybin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psilocybin), the active substance in magic mushrooms, for depression that other treatments have not helped, on November 9th, 2026. A fast-review voucher sets a target of one to two months, and the company hopes to launch in the first half of 2027 ([Psychiatric Times](https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/nda-submission-complete-comp360-psilocybin-for-treatment-resistant-depression)). Compass has moved its own dates before: in October 2024 it pushed back the results of both of its large trials ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/business/compass-delays-pivotal-trial-readouts-for-psilocybin-in-depression-lays-off-30-of-staff)). An approval would not change the site, which already places psychedelic therapy at in clinical use, because Australia has let psychiatrists prescribe psilocybin since July 2023 ([the upgrade's page](/branches/mood/mood-psychedelic-therapy-approved-for-prescription/)).

**Results from the first trial of a treatment that partly resets cells.** The trial registry gives May 2027, as an estimate, for collecting the main results of Life Biosciences' trial in the eye ([the registry entry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07290244)). If a checked, published report showed a sign of age turned back in treated people, compared with untreated ones, this change would reach first human and the count would go to 29 of 35. The trial is built to test safety, with no untreated group, so the trial's results alone are unlikely to show that ([the upgrade's page](/branches/lifespan/lifespan-partially-resetting-cells-reverses-ageing-in-an-animal/)).

**A brain implant built for speed, in its first people.** [Paradromics](https://www.paradromics.com/) says its implant carried more than 200 bits a second in tests in sheep, not yet checked by other scientists ([the company](https://www.paradromics.com/blog/bci-benchmarking)). Its first study in people is recruiting, and the registry estimates May 2027 for the study's main results, which are about safety ([the registry entry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07357428)). A person sending more than 40 bits a second for ten minutes or more, in a checked, published report, would move the site's entry for [a brain link faster than speech](/branches/link/link-brain-link-carries-more-information-than-speech/) from idea only to first human, and the count to 29 of 35.

**A larger trial of pig kidneys.** [eGenesis](https://egenesisbio.com/) has permission from the Food and Drug Administration for a trial of its gene-edited pig kidney in 33 people, and says it expects to start it in the first quarter of 2027 ([the company](https://egenesisbio.com/news-media/press-releases/egenesis-announces-clinical-update-in-ongoing-expanded-access-study-of-egen-2784-in-patients-with-kidney-failure)). Starting a trial would not move pig organs up a stage on the site; a regulator's approval for use outside a trial would.

**Two dates nobody has given.** The [European Medicines Agency](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/homepage) accepted Otarmeni, the hearing gene therapy, for review on May 22nd, 2026, and has not said when it will decide ([The Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/inside-hearing/research/ema-accepts-otarmeni-gene-therapy-for-otof-related-hearing-loss-for-review)). Approval in Europe would bring the therapy to more children, but the site already places it at in clinical use, so it would not move up a stage. And Neuralink's implant for the blind has no date: the last window Musk gave for a first person closed in June 2026 ([the post of October 31st](/news/2026-10-31-musks-claims-for-telepathy-and-blindsight-beside-the-record/)). A first Neuralink implant in a blind person would be news on the site, but it would not move a stage, because an implant in the part of the brain that sees was first tried in a person in 1968 ([the upgrade's page](/branches/senses/senses-implant-in-the-visual-cortex-gives-sight-without-eyes/)).

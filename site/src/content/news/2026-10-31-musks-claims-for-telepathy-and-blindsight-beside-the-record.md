On October 30th, 2026, [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk), who founded and owns the brain-implant company [Neuralink](https://neuralink.com/), wrote two short posts on X, the social network, about its products. [The first](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2116018780111597911), in full: "Neuralink's first product is called Telepathy. It enables high-speed communication with computers or humans simply by thinking. Output bandwidth will vastly exceed the fastest human speech or typing in a few years. Read that sentence again." Four minutes later he quoted it in [a second post](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2116019939056513218): "Our second product is called Blindsight and it will, as the name suggests, enable even those who are completely blind or have never seen to see. It is ready from an engineering standpoint. Just waiting on regulatory approval."

The Upgrade Tree counts how much of a human being can be changed with technology today, from the public record, and it keeps a separate log of what people have claimed about merging with machines, so that the two can be read side by side ([the claims](/claims/)). A claim never moves anything on the tree. Both posts are now in the log, and this post sets each one beside what the record shows, says what would settle it, and says by when.

## Telepathy, and the Speed of Speech

Telepathy is the company's name for what its implant already does in its trials. In Neuralink's own words, from its update of January 28th, 2026, the product "aims to enable people with paralysis to directly control computers, phones, and robotic limbs using their thoughts alone" ([the update](https://neuralink.com/updates/two-years-of-telepathy/)). The same update counts 21 people enrolled in its trials worldwide. Those trials have permission to run in four countries: the United States, where the company has said its first trial runs under an investigational device exemption, the United States permission to test an unapproved device in people, granted by the [Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/) in May 2023 ([Neuralink, September 2023](https://neuralink.com/updates/first-clinical-trial-open-for-recruitment/)); Canada, approved by [Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada.html) in November 2024 ([Neuralink, November 2024](https://neuralink.com/updates/can-prime-study-launch/)); Great Britain, approved by the [Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/medicines-and-healthcare-products-regulatory-agency) and the ethics bodies in July 2025 ([Neuralink, July 2025](https://neuralink.com/updates/gb-prime-study-launch/)); and the United Arab Emirates, where a trial at a hospital in Abu Dhabi began in May 2025 ([the registry entry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06992596)). Those are permissions to run a trial. The company's trials page, and its recent updates, say what they are not: "Neuralink devices are investigational and have not been approved by the FDA or other regulatory authorities" ([the company's trials page](https://neuralink.com/trials/)).

The speed is what Musk's post is about, and every number for it is the company's own. On October 1st, 2026, Neuralink reported that one participant had moved a cursor at 11.32 bits a second, which it called a record for a brain link, that the middle participant's rate is "roughly 10", and that its previous record was 10.39 ([the update](https://neuralink.com/updates/pretraining-on-50000-hours/)). A bit a second is a measure of how much information gets through: on the grid task the company uses, a person with a mouse in their hand scores about 8 to 10 ([the January update](https://neuralink.com/updates/two-years-of-telepathy/)). For text, the January update reports participants typing by imagining the movement of ten fingers at up to 40 words a minute, and says the company's speech trial aims at 140. None of these figures is in a peer-reviewed report, which is a paper checked by other scientists before it is published. I looked for one and found none: the one journal item from the trials, dated October 23rd, 2026, is an abstract in the proceedings of a conference on motor neurone disease, printed in the journal Muscle & Nerve, not a full paper ([the journal's record](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.70436)).

The two yardsticks Musk named have measured values. Speech carries about 39 bits of information a second, measured across 17 languages by Christophe Coupé and colleagues at the French national research agency and the University of Lyon ([the paper, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaw2594)). An advanced typist produces about 120 words a minute, which the neuroscientists Jieyu Zheng and Markus Meister at the [California Institute of Technology](https://www.caltech.edu/) put at about 10 bits a second ([the paper, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.11.008)). So the company's best cursor figure would have to more than triple to pass speech, and its typing figure would have to triple to pass a fast typist, and either would then have to be measured in a way other people can check. One caution the record itself carries: a rate of pointing and a rate of language are not the same measure, and the tree's speed chart keeps them on separate lines ([the link-to-machines branch](/branches/link/#bandwidth)). The comparison here is Musk's, because his post names speech and typing as the things to beat.

The tree's own number for a brain link is the best peer-reviewed rate, and it is unchanged: 4.16 bits a second for a cursor, from [Stanford](https://www.stanford.edu/) in 2017, and 78 words a minute for text, from the [University of California, San Francisco](https://www.ucsf.edu/) in 2023, with Neuralink's own 2024 figure of 8.0 drawn beside them and marked as the company's measurement ([the speed chart](/branches/link/#bandwidth)). The upgrade that Musk's claim would settle is on the same branch: [a brain link that carries more information a second than speech](/branches/link/link-brain-link-carries-more-information-than-speech/), which stands at idea only. What would count, as the tree wrote it down before these posts, is a sustained rate above 40 bits a second over at least ten minutes, in a peer-reviewed report that says how the rate was worked out, and not reconstructed from attempted speech alone. "In a few years" from October 2026 is read here as by about 2029 to 2030, and that is the date the log now carries beside the post ([the claim](/claims/#2026-10-30-musk-x-telepathy-bandwidth)). Zheng and Meister, who are in the log as critics, predict that no link will beat about 10 bits a second, because that is all the deliberate part of a person does ([their entry](/claims/#2024-12-17-zheng-meister-the-musk-illusion)). One measured number above 39 would settle both.

## Blindsight, and the Word "Approval"

Blindsight is an implant for the part of the brain that sees, for people whose eyes or optic nerves no longer work. The company's one statement about it on its own site is from September 17th, 2024: "We have received Breakthrough Device Designation from the FDA for Blindsight, for individuals with vision impairment" ([Neuralink's post](https://neuralink.com/updates/neuralink-receives-breakthrough-device-designation-for-blindsight/)). The news agency [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/) reported the same day that Musk had said on X that the device "will enable even those who have lost both eyes and their optic nerve to see", and that the company had not said when it would move into human trials ([the Reuters report, carried by The Globe and Mail](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/business/international-business/article-musks-neuralink-gets-fdas-breakthrough-device-tag-for-blindsight)).

A breakthrough designation is not an approval. In the Food and Drug Administration's own description, the Breakthrough Devices Program is a voluntary scheme for devices that could treat a life-threatening or irreversibly debilitating condition, meant to speed up "development, assessment, and review", and the designated devices "must meet the FDA's rigorous standards for device safety and effectiveness in order to be authorized for marketing" ([the program's page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/how-study-and-market-your-device/breakthrough-devices-program)). Nor is it permission to put the device in a person: in the United States that is a separate permission, the investigational device exemption, which is what the company's paralysis trial has had since May 2023 ([Neuralink, September 2023](https://neuralink.com/updates/first-clinical-trial-open-for-recruitment/)).

What the record shows for Blindsight, then, is a device that no regulator has cleared for a trial on the public record, let alone for sale. Neuralink's trials page lists two active trials, one for controlling devices and one for speech, and none for sight ([the trials page](https://neuralink.com/trials/)). The United States trial registry, searched on October 31st, 2026, lists seven Neuralink studies, every one for people with paralysis or loss of speech ([the registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?spons=Neuralink)). No person is known to have the device. Musk has given dates before: in June 2025 he said "In the next six to 12 months, we'll be doing our first implants for vision" ([MobiHealthNews, June 23rd, 2025](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/elon-musk-provides-update-blindsight-implant-trials)), a window that closed in June 2026, and the news service [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/) reported in July 2025, from the company's own investor documents, that Neuralink anticipates launching Blindsight by 2030 ([the Reuters account of Bloomberg's report](https://www.aol.com/news/neuralink-targets-1-billion-revenue-220503712.html)).

On the tree, the change Blindsight would be is [an implant in the visual cortex that gives sight with no working eyes](/branches/senses/senses-implant-in-the-visual-cortex-gives-sight-without-eyes/), and it has stood at first human since 1968, when [Giles Brindley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giles_Brindley) and Walpole Lewin reported a blind woman who saw spots of light where electrodes over her visual cortex were driven ([the 1968 paper](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1968.sp008519)). Camera-driven versions followed, and in 2021 a woman in Spain recognised shapes and letters through a hundred-electrode array, but the pictures are still a few dozen spots, and nothing is approved ([the upgrade's page](/branches/senses/senses-implant-in-the-visual-cortex-gives-sight-without-eyes/)). The next stage up, in clinical use, needs a regulator's approval record. Musk's post goes further than the record in one more way: it says the device will give sight to people who "have never seen", and the tree's record holds no report of a person blind from birth seeing through such an implant.

What would settle this claim is written beside it in the log ([the claim](/claims/#2026-10-30-musk-x-blindsight-ready)): a regulator's approval record, which would also move the upgrade one stage, and a peer-reviewed report of what the first patients actually see. A first implant in a person would be news on this site, but it would not move the upgrade, which has had its first human for 58 years.

## What Moved

Nothing on the tree moved. The headline count stays at [28 of the 35](/data/headline.json) changes the site follows having been tried in a person, the best peer-reviewed rate for a brain link stays where it was ([the speed chart](/branches/link/#bandwidth)), and the implant for sight stays at first human. What changed is the log: two more dated claims, each with the exact words, the record beside them, and the date by which a regulator's record or a measured number will show whether they were right.
